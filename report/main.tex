\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
One-pixel attacks illustrate that image classifiers can be fooled in a black-box setting by perturbing a single pixel.
This project revisits the Differential Evolution (DE) one-pixel attack on a pretrained CIFAR-10 classifier and studies how successful attacks affect post-hoc explanations.
For RQ1, explanation stability between clean and adversarial inputs is measured using IoU@10 and Spearman rank correlation, and explanation faithfulness is assessed via deletion AUC.
Across successful attacks, stability typically decreases and deletion AUC tends to degrade; Integrated Gradients is generally more stable than Grad-CAM and RISE under one-pixel perturbations.
For RQ2, two guidance strategies are evaluated: a fast-prior multi-target variant and a responsibility-map-guided DE that uses RISE saliency to bias mutation.
At 10k scale (9{,}213 eligible images), untargeted DE achieves 37.7\% image-wise success with median query costs of 6{,}000 among successful attacks and 40{,}400 over all eligible images.
Fast-prior reduces image-wise success to 14.2\% and increases the median successful query cost to 70{,}848, while improving explanation stability among successful cases.
RISE-guided DE matches the untargeted success rate at similar overall cost but slightly higher median successful query cost (6{,}400), indicating that simple saliency guidance does not improve the success--cost trade-off in this configuration.
All reported numbers, plots, and tables are generated from a frozen results pack to ensure reproducibility.

\section{Conclusions and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project investigated one-pixel adversarial attacks on image classifiers under a black-box threat model, with two research questions: (RQ1) how successful one-pixel attacks affect explanation stability and deletion-based faithfulness, and (RQ2) whether responsibility-map guidance improves the success--cost trade-off and how it affects explanation stability among successful adversarial examples.

\subsection{Summary of findings}

\textbf{RQ1 (effect on explanations).}
Across successful one-pixel adversarial examples, explanation stability decreases relative to the clean input for all three methods studied (Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients, and RISE), and deletion-based faithfulness also degrades on average.
Integrated Gradients is the most stable explanation method under this evaluation protocol, while Grad-CAM and RISE show larger changes in top-ranked pixels and attribution ordering.

\textbf{RQ2 (guidance vs baseline).}
Three attack configurations were compared at 10k scale: untargeted Differential Evolution (DE), a fastprior multi-target variant, and RISE-guided DE.
The untargeted baseline achieves an image-wise success rate of 0.3774 on 9{,}213 eligible images, with median query cost 40{,}400 over all eligible images and 6{,}000 among successful attacks.
Fastprior reduces image-wise success to 0.1422 and has a target-wise success rate of 1.8\% (1{,}482 successful targets out of 82{,}917 target attempts); its median query cost is 70{,}848 both overall and among successful images because of its fixed-budget design.
RISE-guided DE attains the same image-wise success rate as untargeted DE (0.3774), with the same median overall query cost (40{,}400) and a slightly higher median successful query cost (6{,}400), suggesting that simple responsibility-map guidance does not improve efficiency in this configuration.

Among successful adversarial examples, fastprior yields higher explanation stability across all three explainers, whereas RISE-guided DE remains close to baseline for Grad-CAM and Integrated Gradients but lowers RISE-based stability.
This indicates that guidance changes the \emph{distribution of successful cases}, which can affect explanation metrics even when the perturbation budget remains fixed to a single pixel.

\subsection{Limitations}

The evaluation uses a single dataset (CIFAR-10) and a fixed pretrained architecture, so results may not generalise to higher-resolution datasets, different model families, or other threat models.
Explanation metrics are computed only on successful adversarial examples; therefore, stability and faithfulness estimates reflect both explanation sensitivity and the selection effects induced by each attack configuration.

\subsection{Future work}

Future work can strengthen both the scientific conclusions and the practical utility of guidance:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Broader model coverage:} replicate on additional CIFAR-10 architectures and at least one higher-resolution dataset to test whether the observed stability trends persist.
  \item \textbf{Improved guidance mechanisms:} replace the current heuristic saliency integration with principled priors or hybrid objectives, for example multi-objective DE balancing target confidence and saliency alignment.
  \item \textbf{Query-efficiency analysis:} characterise the full cost distribution, not only medians, including tail behaviour and early-stopping sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{Robustness and defences:} evaluate whether standard robustness techniques such as adversarial training or input preprocessing change explanation stability under one-pixel attacks.
\end{itemize}

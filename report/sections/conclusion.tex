\section{Conclusion and future work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project investigated one-pixel adversarial attacks on image classifiers under a black-box threat model, with two linked aims:
first, to study how successful one-pixel attacks affect post-hoc explanations; and second, to test whether responsibility-map guidance improves one-pixel search in a meaningful empirical sense.
The work was designed as a controlled, reproducible experimental study rather than as a search for the strongest possible attack under every configuration.

\subsection{Answer to RQ1}
RQ1 asked how successful one-pixel attacks affect explanations.
The results show that successful one-pixel adversarial examples are associated with measurable explanation change under all three explanation methods considered.
Integrated Gradients is the most stable method under the present evaluation protocol, both in top-ranked overlap and in rank correlation.
Grad-CAM is less stable, and RISE is least stable in rank-correlation terms.
Deletion-based faithfulness also decreases on average for all three methods under successful attack.

The main conclusion from RQ1 is therefore not simply that explanations ``change,'' but that explanation robustness under sparse adversarial perturbation is clearly method-dependent.
A successful one-pixel perturbation can leave one explanation method relatively stable while shifting another more substantially.
That makes explanation choice an important part of adversarial analysis rather than a neutral visualisation decision.

\subsection{Answer to RQ2}
RQ2 asked whether responsibility-map guidance can improve one-pixel attacks.
The results show that the answer, in the tested configuration, is no in the strong sense of improving the overall success--cost frontier.
The RISE-guided DE variant matches the untargeted baseline in image-wise success rate but does not reduce overall median query cost and slightly increases the median cost among successful attacks.
Fastprior, meanwhile, improves explanation stability on its successful subset but does so together with substantially lower image-wise success and much higher cost.

The main conclusion from RQ2 is therefore that guidance changes the \emph{type} of successful adversarial cases more readily than it improves the attack frontier itself.
A saliency-derived prior may bias the search toward more explanation-stable successes, but that does not automatically make the attack procedure better.
In the present benchmark setting, simple responsibility-map guidance is not enough to produce a cleaner success--cost trade-off than untargeted DE.

\subsection{Overall contribution}
The contribution of the project is not a new explanation method or a state-of-the-art black-box attack.
Its main contribution is a tightly integrated comparative framework in which sparse adversarial success, query cost, explanation stability, and deletion-based faithfulness are analysed together on a fixed 10k evaluation set.
That contribution is strengthened by the use of frozen artefacts, generated figures and tables, and explicit claims-to-evidence mapping, all of which improve auditability and reduce transcription risk.

More broadly, the project helps clarify an important point that can be obscured in more compact attack studies:
a one-pixel attack should not be judged only by whether it succeeds.
It should also be judged by how it succeeds, what it costs, and what kind of explanation behaviour accompanies that success.

\subsection{Main limitations}
The conclusions are bounded by the scope of the evaluation.
The experiments use one dataset, one main pretrained architecture, one sparse black-box threat model, and three explanation methods.
Explanation analysis is performed on successful adversarial subsets only, which introduces selection and composition effects.
The reported metrics are informative proxies rather than direct measures of internal model reasoning.
These limitations do not remove the value of the findings, but they define the level at which the results should be interpreted.

\subsection{Future work}
There are several natural directions for future work.

First, the strongest immediate extension is broader validation across additional architectures and at least one higher-resolution dataset.
That would test whether the explanation-stability ordering and the guidance trade-offs observed here persist beyond the CIFAR-10/ResNet-20 setting.

Second, guidance itself can be improved.
The present report evaluates one simple black-box saliency prior.
Future work could study more principled spatial priors, hybrid objectives, or multi-objective search procedures that balance adversarial confidence, cost, and explanation-aware constraints.

Third, fuller distributional analysis would strengthen the understanding of attack cost.
The report already uses medians and successful/all-image splits, but richer analysis of tails, failure modes, and class-conditional behaviour could reveal structure hidden by aggregate summaries.

Finally, the interaction between adversarial robustness and explanation robustness deserves broader investigation.
The present project shows that explanation behaviour can shift substantially under extremely sparse successful perturbations.
A natural next question is whether defences or robustness-oriented training procedures change not only attack success, but also the stability and faithfulness of explanations under attack.

\subsection{Final closing statement}
Taken together, the results show that one-pixel attacks remain a valuable experimental lens for studying both adversarial vulnerability and explanation behaviour.
In this benchmark setting, successful sparse black-box attacks are common enough to be meaningful, explanation robustness is clearly method-dependent, and simple saliency-based guidance does not improve the success--cost frontier.
Those findings are modest in scope, but they are clear, reproducible, and empirically grounded.
